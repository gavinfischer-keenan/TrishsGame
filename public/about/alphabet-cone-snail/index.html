Behind the name

The alphabet cone snail.

A shell that invites a second look.

Conus spurius Gmelin, 1791

Patterns to ponder

The alphabet cone is a marine snail, Conus spurius, in the cone-snail family Conidae. Its pale shell carries rows of orange-brown marks. Seen up close, those little patches can suggest handwritten characters: a natural invitation to look for familiar shapes. The resemblance is in the observer's eye; it is not an alphabet written by the animal. WoRMS: accepted name ↗ National Shell Museum: alphabet cone ↗

A brief first swim

This western Atlantic species lives in shallow marine waters. Research describes it as a predator of marine worms, with possible mollusc prey too. Its earliest journey is surprisingly short: larvae hatch from egg capsules and can swim for only minutes to hours before settling to the bottom. Researchers studying specimens from Sanibel Island documented these large hatchlings and their transition toward life on the seafloor. Leal, Kohn & Mensch, 2017 ↗

Why Word Wager?

Word Wager borrows the alphabet cone's visual association with letters. Finding a possible character among the shell's markings echoes the pleasure of spotting a word among possibilities. The connection is playful: the snail's pattern supplies the inspiration, while the wordplay belongs to the game.
