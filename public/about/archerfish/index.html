Behind the name

The archerfish.

A small hunter with remarkable aim.

Toxotes — the archerfishes

A family of sharpshooters

“Archerfish” covers multiple species in the genus Toxotes, including the banded archerfish pictured here and the sevenspot archerfish. They belong to the family Toxotidae. The sevenspot species inhabits fresh water and estuaries across much of Southeast Asia and northern Australia. Australian Museum: archerfishes ↗ Sevenspot natural history ↗

A jet aimed above the surface

Archerfish can knock insects from overhanging vegetation with a precisely directed jet of water. A groove in the roof of the mouth and a matching ridge on the tongue help form the water’s passage. Swimming near the surface gives these hunters a view of prey overhead. Australian Museum: feeding and adaptations ↗

Aiming across the air–water boundary involves refraction: light bends, changing where a target appears. Experiments show that archerfish can adjust their shots after airflow deflects the jet. Separate research shows they change mouth-opening dynamics to shape jets for different target distances. These are measured hunting abilities; they do not imply conscious calculation. Volotsky et al., 2024 ↗ Gerullis & Schuster, 2014 ↗

Why Archerfish?

Archerfish: Fleet tactics borrows the animal’s association with careful aim. The fleet, ships, and tactical encounters are our game’s metaphor. The real fish hunts food, and the stylized logo does not identify a particular species.
