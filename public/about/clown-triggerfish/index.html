Behind the name

The clown triggerfish.

Bold spots, a hidden reef anchor.

Balistoides conspicillum

A reef fish dressed in dots

The clown triggerfish, Balistoides conspicillum, belongs to the triggerfish family, Balistidae. Its dark body carries large white spots across the lower head and flanks, while bright lips and yellow markings sharpen the contrast. It lives on coral reefs of the Indo-West Pacific; the Australian Museum records it from offshore Western Australian reefs and the Great Barrier Reef south to northern New South Wales. Australian Museum ↗

A spine that locks

When sheltering, it can raise its first dorsal spine and lock it with a smaller spine behind it, wedging itself into a reef crevice. The same anchoring trick helps it rest at night. Powerful jaws and strong teeth tackle crustaceans, molluscs, and sea urchins. Waikīkī Aquarium ↗

Why Clown Triggerfish?

Clown Triggerfish — Four in a Row borrows the fish’s striking spotted appearance for a game of colored counters. The logo’s counter colors are playful symbols: the living animal has its own distinctive pattern, and the connection is a visual metaphor for the game.
