Pistol Shrimp.

Meet the namesake

Small shrimp. Big snap.

Pistol shrimp, also called snapping shrimp, belong to the family Alpheidae. The name covers many species with different colors and habits. Our illustrated character celebrates that whole group; the photograph shows one real example, the tiger snapping shrimp. [1]

Their signature trick starts with an oversized claw. As it snaps shut, it ejects a fast jet of water that creates a cavitation bubble. The bubble collapses and produces the sharp sound: the noise comes from that collapse, rather than the claw surfaces banging together. Researchers demonstrated the timing using high-speed images and underwater sound measurements. [2]

Many live in shallow tropical and subtropical waters, especially around coral reefs. Their smaller claw helps with foraging and burrowing, while their diets vary with species and habitat. [1]

Certain pistol shrimp share a burrow with a goby. The shrimp maintains the shelter; the fish guards it, and the partners communicate through touch. This partnership belongs to particular shrimp and gobies, rather than every pistol shrimp species. [3]

That tiny creature's outsized snap is the spirit behind Pistol Shrimp: BrickSmack.
